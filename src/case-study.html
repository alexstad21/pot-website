Case Study

Airspace disruption & data resilience.

When sudden airspace closures cause travel records to diverge across airline, GDS, and consolidator systems, an independent verification protocol becomes the authoritative timeline.

The problem

Mass airspace closures — whether caused by geopolitical events, natural phenomena, or technical incidents — produce a cascade of partial cancellations, rebookings, and refund flows. In the hours and days that follow, the same ticket can appear with different statuses across:

  • The airline's departure control system
  • The GDS booking record
  • The consolidator's mid-office
  • The agency's back-office
  • The settlement framework's reconciliation files

That divergence is operationally normal — each system is doing its job — but it makes downstream processes (insurance claims, regulatory reporting, customer disputes, chargeback defence) difficult and slow.

The PoT contribution

Proof of Travel does not try to be another reconciliation engine. Instead, it produces a thin, deterministic, independently verifiable timeline of ticket lifecycle events. When source systems agree, a proof is generated and anchored. When they disagree, no proof is generated — and the absence of a proof is itself a useful operational signal.

What this looks like in practice

  • Issuance proofs show that a ticket was, in fact, issued — useful when downstream systems lose context.
  • Departure proofs confirm the leg was operated, settling the most common dispute category.
  • Reconciliation proofs confirm that settlement signals from independent sources converged — a high-quality input to insurance and chargeback workflows.
  • Refund proofs confirm that funds movement and ticket-state changes are operationally consistent.

Outcomes

In the disruption scenarios we have modelled and operated against:

  • Insurance claim resolution time is materially shortened because the timeline is no longer in dispute.
  • Chargeback defence improves because acquirers receive a public, verifiable reference instead of an internal claim.
  • Audit and regulatory reporting becomes simpler because the underlying event data has an independent attestation.
  • Customer dispute load decreases because the operational timeline is consistent across stakeholders.

Why an independent layer matters

A reconciliation layer owned by any single participant — airline, GDS, agency, settlement framework — is structurally unsuited to settle disputes between participants. PoT is independent by design. It introduces no new trusted third party; instead, the proof itself is verifiable using only public information and a deterministic re-derivation.
