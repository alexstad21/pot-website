Case Studies

Real-world proof of travel in action.

Five short scenarios showing how an independent, cryptographically verifiable evidence layer holds up across individual verification, corporate audit, dispute resolution, and ecosystem-level oversight.

Proof of Travel (PoT) is an independent verification protocol designed to provide long-term, cryptographically verifiable evidence of travel events — without relying on a single system, organisation, or database.

The following case studies illustrate how PoT can be used in practice across different scenarios, including individual verification, corporate audit, dispute resolution, and ecosystem-level oversight.

These examples are intended to demonstrate:

  • how independent verification works,
  • what PoT does — and does not — record,
  • and why a neutral verification layer becomes more valuable as systems evolve over time.

Each case focuses on a specific problem that exists today in travel operations and compliance, and shows how PoT provides objective, privacy-preserving evidence that can be verified independently — now and in the future.

Proof of Travel does not replace existing systems. It complements them by providing a durable reference layer that outlives platforms, vendors, and formats.

Case Study #1

Verifying a journey — without paperwork

Paperwork can fail. Independent proof shouldn't.

This is how Proof of Travel verifies a journey — without relying on screenshots or PDFs.

Case Study #2

Independent verification during a corporate audit

This scenario illustrates how Proof of Travel can be used during a corporate audit to independently verify that travel activity occurred as recorded, without relying on internal screenshots or manual exports.

PoT provides auditors with a neutral, verifiable reference that exists outside corporate and supplier systems.

Case Study #3

A verification layer that outlasts platforms

This example demonstrates how Proof of Travel remains verifiable even when legacy booking, airline, or consolidator systems are upgraded, merged, or retired.

PoT is designed as a long-lived verification layer that outlasts platforms, vendors, and formats.

Case Study #4

Dispute resolution with neutral evidence

This case explores a common dispute scenario where different systems present conflicting versions of the same travel record.

Proof of Travel does not resolve disputes, but provides independent evidence of what was recorded and when — enabling objective verification without trusting a single party's data.

Case Study #5

Ecosystem-level oversight across issuers

This scenario shows how Proof of Travel can support ecosystem-level oversight across multiple issuers, platforms, and systems.

By anchoring cryptographic commitments outside issuer infrastructure, PoT enables independent verification while preserving operational autonomy and data privacy.
